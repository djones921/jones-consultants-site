01

Project Engineering & Owner's Representation

Most owners don't have a spare engineer to run a capital project. JCS Engineering fills that seat: reviewing design, questioning assumptions, driving the schedule, and holding every other party to the intent of the project — with no financial stake in the construction contract.

We act as the owner's single point of contact across the architect-engineer, construction manager, equipment vendors, and quality organization, so decisions get made once, on the record, by someone who answers to you.

When to engage

  • You have capital approved but no internal engineer to own it
  • Your operations or quality staff can't absorb a project on top of production
  • You want independent eyes on the A/E and contractor
  • A project has drifted and needs a reset

Typical deliverables

  • Project execution plan
  • Weekly status & decision log
  • Budget and schedule tracking
  • Design review comments
  • Meeting leadership & minutes
  • Closeout & lessons learned
02

Capital Project Lifecycle Management

Capital projects fail in the gaps between phases — a basis of design that never made it into the URS, a procurement decision that quietly invalidated the validation plan. Lifecycle management means owning those transitions, not just the phases.

We structure projects around defined stage gates with explicit deliverables and go/no-go criteria, so leadership sees a decision at each gate rather than a surprise at the end.

When to engage

  • A multi-year project that needs one continuous owner
  • Multiple vendors and firms whose scopes must interlock
  • Stage-gate governance is required for capital approval

Typical deliverables

  • Stage-gate framework & criteria
  • Integrated master schedule
  • Deliverables register
  • Gate review packages
  • Phase handover checklists
03

Conceptual & Front-End Design Support

The cheapest change on any project is the one made before drawings exist. Front-end work is where capacity, layout, flows, and utilities get settled — and where an owner's engineer earns the most for the least.

We translate operational and quality requirements into a defensible basis of design and user requirement specifications, and pressure-test concepts against constructability, GMP flows, and real budgets.

When to engage

  • Evaluating a new line, suite, or site
  • Preparing a capital request that needs a credible estimate
  • An A/E's concept needs an owner-side technical review

Typical deliverables

  • Basis of design (BOD)
  • User requirement specifications (URS)
  • Block flow & adjacency studies
  • Option comparison & recommendation
  • Estimate review & risk contingency input
04

Construction Administration & Oversight

Drawings describe intent. Construction is where intent meets a jobsite — and where an owner without field presence learns about problems after they're expensive.

We manage the submittal and RFI flow, walk the work, track progress against the schedule and pay applications, and coordinate construction around live operations so production isn't collateral damage.

When to engage

  • Construction inside or adjacent to an operating GMP facility
  • A GC or CM you'd like independently verified
  • Complex MEP, cleanroom, or process installations

Typical deliverables

  • Submittal & RFI logs
  • Field observation reports
  • Progress and pay-app verification
  • Change order review
  • Punch list management
  • Turnover package review
05

CQV Execution

Commissioning, qualification, and validation is where projects either become production assets or stall for months. It rewards planning that started during design — impact assessments, test strategies, and documentation that traces cleanly back to requirements.

We plan and execute CQV from commissioning plan through IQ, OQ, and PQ, working with your quality unit so the handover package stands up to inspection and your teams can run the system on day one.

When to engage

  • New or modified equipment, utilities, or facilities entering GMP use
  • Validation resources are thin relative to project load
  • You want CQV integrated with construction rather than tacked on

Typical deliverables

  • Commissioning & qualification plan
  • System impact / risk assessments
  • FAT / SAT protocols & execution
  • IQ / OQ / PQ protocols and reports
  • Requirements traceability matrix
  • Turnover & validation summary reports
06

Change Management & Risk Mitigation

Every project changes. The question is whether change is managed deliberately — assessed, priced, approved, and documented — or absorbed silently until it shows up as a schedule slip or a compliance gap.

We run a disciplined change process and a risk register that's actually reviewed, so leadership sees exposure early and decisions are made with the full picture.

When to engage

  • Scope is evolving while work is underway
  • Regulatory or quality impact of changes must be documented
  • Multiple stakeholders need a single approval path

Typical deliverables

  • Change control procedure
  • Change log with impact & cost
  • Risk register & mitigation plans
  • Escalation framework
  • Regular risk reviews
07

Process Optimization & Tech Transfer

Tech transfer is engineering work disguised as paperwork. Equipment differences, utility capacities, cleaning strategies, and room classifications all have to be reconciled before a process runs where it hasn't before.

We support the gap assessment, facility-fit evaluation, and equipment and utility modifications that make a transfer succeed, and apply the same lens to optimizing processes already in place.

When to engage

  • Scaling a process up or moving it to a new suite or site
  • Facility fit for an incoming product
  • Throughput or reliability improvements on an existing line

Typical deliverables

  • Facility & equipment fit-gap assessment
  • Process flow & utility demand analysis
  • Modification scope and estimate
  • Transfer execution support
  • Optimization recommendations
08

Facility Expansion & Master Planning

Expansion decided one project at a time produces facilities that fight themselves: utilities at capacity, flows that cross, and the next project always harder than the last.

We develop site and facility master plans that phase growth logically, protect current operations during construction, and give leadership a capital roadmap rather than a series of one-off requests.

When to engage

  • Anticipated capacity growth over several years
  • A site with constrained utilities or footprint
  • Sequencing multiple projects around continuous production

Typical deliverables

  • Capacity & demand model
  • Site & utility master plan
  • Phasing and sequencing strategy
  • Order-of-magnitude capital roadmap
09

Regulatory & GMP Compliance Readiness

Compliance isn't a phase. Requirements traceability, design documentation, and change records either accumulate cleanly throughout a project or get reconstructed painfully before an inspection.

We build GMP thinking into project governance from the start — how documents are controlled, how changes are assessed, how the design is justified — so the facility is inspection-ready when it's production-ready.

When to engage

  • A facility or system will be subject to regulatory inspection
  • Documentation practices need to be established or repaired
  • Quality and engineering need a shared framework on a project

Typical deliverables

  • Document control plan
  • Design qualification support
  • Traceability approach
  • Inspection readiness assessment
  • Remediation plans
10

Continuous Improvement & Post-Project Support

Handover isn't the finish line — it's the first day systems run under real conditions. The first months surface the tuning, training gaps, and small fixes that decide whether a project is remembered as a success.

We stay engaged through startup and stabilization, close out open items, and capture lessons that make the next project cheaper and faster.

When to engage

  • Systems recently turned over to operations
  • Persistent punch list or performance issues
  • Building an internal playbook for future projects

Typical deliverables

  • Startup & stabilization support
  • Punch list closure
  • Performance review
  • Lessons-learned report
  • Operational readiness assessment
